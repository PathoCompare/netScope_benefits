# netScope Product Comparison

| Product | Main advantage | Main limitation | Compared with netScope |
|---|---|---|---|
| **netScope** | Broad format support combined with viewing, annotation and networking | Windows-oriented desktop experience | **Strong balance of viewing, compatibility and sharing** |
| **QuPath** | Excellent analysis capabilities and open-source ecosystem | Collaboration and server functions are less integrated | Better for advanced analysis, less complete as one integrated environment |
| **3DHISTECH SlideViewer** | Good experience with 3DHISTECH workflows | More vendor-focused | netScope is broader across scanner formats |
| **Hamamatsu NDP.view2** | Very good native Hamamatsu experience | More limited outside its main ecosystem | netScope is more flexible for mixed datasets |
| **ZEN lite** | Strong ZEISS integration | More focused on ZEISS workflows | netScope has a broader multi-vendor focus |
| **ASAP** | Lightweight and open | Smaller ecosystem and fewer integrated management features | netScope offers a more complete product family |
| **OpenSlide** | Excellent format-reading foundation | Not a complete end-user viewer | netScope turns broad format support into a finished application |

## Assessment

The ratings are a qualitative assessment rather than benchmark scores.

netScope's main advantage is the combination of a broad WSI viewer with integrated sharing and server options. QuPath remains particularly strong for advanced image analysis, while vendor-specific viewers can have an advantage when working primarily with their own scanner ecosystem.

Overall, netScope offers a good balance between compatibility, viewing functionality and infrastructure. It is not necessarily the best tool for every individual task, but it is one of the more complete options when the complete workflow is considered.
